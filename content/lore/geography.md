## Inazria

Inhabited by hardy farmers and nomadic cattle herders, Inazria is a coastal country east of Kystriland separated by the Violet Sea.

The central part of the country is spacious, fertile and tropical. The region is split between tropical forests to the west and dry, temperate plains to the east by the Marram mountain range. Further north, in the thin sliver of foothills sandwiched between the Marram and the Bisengal border lies the small remnants of the Palcha kingdom.

Between the great cities lies a peninsula thick with sprawling towns, each with their own flavors and customs.

North of Windfall, the climate rapidly becomes temperate and sparsely populated. The coastline quickly turns eastward and northward, racing towards the flattening edge of the Marram mountains. A small region exists just west of the northern Marram mountain tip where the soil is quite fertile; the Cahokari elves make their home here. Though the temperature drops quickly as ships sail north from here, the large, mysterious island Andasena appears.

To walk an Inazrian city is to be surrounded by abundance.

### Thilidan

The capital of Inazria is at Thilidan, the largest city in the known world built upon the Thilidan River, in the southern part of the country. Thilidan's sprawling metropolis is considered the largest and most culturally-significant in the world. The city invites countless opportunities of every kind for every race of man. Yet, the recent population expansion of the 4th Generation has created an imbalance of uneasiness in the population. Communities of immigrants and impoverished alike dwell low to the ground near the river, which rises and falls on a daily basis. These places are filled with makeshift dwellings and repurposed structures of bygone eras. The river steps fill at dawn with washers, ferrymen, and morning prayers from thousands of worshippers; the nearby terraces perfume whole districts with spices, herbs and fresh market meats. On the contrary, families of a higher status live above the ground and away from the river where their luxuries can be enjoyed peacefully. A city of extremes, Thilidan harbors the world's wealthiest families living meters from the largest slums in the world.

Inazrian meals are adapted from all of Azergad's cuisines, plus Inazri-style flatbread, rice and sweets made of milk and honey. However, all business dealings must wait for the breaking of bread before prices are discussed. Cutting to the chase and talking shop (like the Dwarves) is no way to establish a trustworthy business relationship. 

### Marlis

The crossroads city of Marlis is a traveler's preferred path through the rugged Marram to Bisengal in the northeast. The city lies within a huge undermountain corridor connecting two sides of the Marram in a particularly thin stretch of the mountain range. Since the city is completely underground, the path is naturally protected from the otherwise harsh conditions on the outside. Inazria's hub of ideation and creation, Marlis was founded next to huge natural deposits of materials like iron, copper, coal, and rare gryxite ore. The border to Bisengal is marked just east of the city where the mountains settle. 

The city rings with the heavy clanging of metals, its smiths famed from the Kystrilands to Khurz-Marn. 

### Windfall

Along the gulf to the northwest, Windfall is home to traders and pioneers amidst a largely unexplored temperate landscape. The city smells of tar, salty winds and roasting coffee; its bustling harbor is crowded with packed trading rigs from every Realm. The coast's arable wetlands provide a wonderful blueprint for irrigated farmlands. Home to many humans, halflings, Dalvas and orcs, Windfall boasts a reputable hub for naval trade across the Violet Sea. The thick, flat jungle landscape inland is known for numerous small lakes and rivers as well as deep caverns shrouded in mystery. Rural villagers tell stories of vast sprawling dungeons and tunnels filled with unthinkably hostile adversaries.

### Other Communities of Inazria

South of Thilidan, along the capital's warm climbing hills, stands the large community of Surakell: their unique ecosystem allows for the growth of world-famous Jeedha tea.

Further inland, where the Thilidan River bends north through groves and busy roads, lies Padramat. Locals wash in the river steps and leave lotus petals for the local deities on the last night of the month. Padramat's shrines are many, as it is a common destination for pilgrimages.

On the gulf road south of Windfall sits Rimah, where coffee roasting houses are open at all hours of the day and night. A typical houseguest of a Rimah townsperson must be offered three cups of coffee (a result of the Rimah brewing method). Refusing the third cup is a clear message of rejection and is considered particularly rude towards the host. It is known in Rimah that Kelanari dislike coffee and Dwarves drink too much of it.

South of Marlis, where the tropical forest thins into cattle country along the dry plains, the drover market of Dharfel gathers twice a month under striped awnings. Herders brand their stock with family seals cut in classical Inazri letters. For a few days, the town smells of grilled meat, dust, and the sharp green of crushed herbs as thousands gather to exchange Human, Orcish, Dalvas and Halfling goods.

Along the sparsely-populated northern coast beyond Windfall's suburban farms huddles the cloistered fishing villages collectively called Nalukash. Nets are blessed each season with salts and ash. Due to the rugged terrain and the high rates of storms and other disasters, Nalukash boats have become prized for their high quality and durability. Rural folk tell stories of cavern mouths inland from Nalukash that breathe cold air, even amidst the sweltering summer - what is rumored to wait at the depths of such caverns, however, elder fishermen prefer not to say.

## Azelliot

To the east of Thilidan lies Azelliot, the breadbasket of Azergad. Most of the land is a flat plain supporting temperate forests full of life. North Azelliot becomes a warm and humid marshland as it approaches the Marram Mountains towards the Bisengal border. As Azelliot stretches southeastward, the landscape splits up into cold islands before reaching the tundra lands at the "bottom of the world".

Azelliot itself is shaped somewhat like one of the great horns the Dalvas sound across the fields.

### The Vash

Its narrow 'mouthpiece' in the northwest is called the Vash, a foothill country gathered beneath the Marram.

### The Luba

From there the land swells into a long throat called the Luba, whose grasslands and grainfields are the breadbasket's beating heart. Drowning at its northern edge, the Luba is swallowed by the fens that border Zilwynbar.

### The Zavu

The 'horn' of Azelliot opens up like a wide bell flung toward the southeast: a region called the Zavu - where the plains fall toward the cold edge of the world. The Zavu remains a mysterious and dangerous region, where the threat of roaming undead leaves the furthest edges to be fully explored.

Toward the southeast, where Azelliot's plains pour open toward the bell of the realm, the warmth begins to fall. The soil turns cold and hard, crops will not take, and life thins away to nothing all the way to the bottom of the world. It is from that lifeless cold that the dead are said to rise.

### The Shava

Along the realm's southwestern flank, where the rolling country breaks against the Veldt, lies the shaded frontier of the Shava. The Shava Dalvas of this frontier are the great guides of the Veldt crossings, leading caravans along the roads of ropes strung between the upper and lower layers to link Azelliot with Inazria, Avarazhan, and the shores beyond.

## The Veldt

Thilidan and central Avarazhan are split by the Veldt, an impressively dense forest of extremely tall trees famously home to the Veldtwin elves. The trees of the Veldt are unlike anything else in the world. Trees of the Veldt grow very tightly together despite being massive: most of multiple kilometers tall, trunks are the sizes of buildings, and leaves collectively make sturdy platforms to move across. There are famously 4 seperate vertical levels of the Veldt (called layers). The ground layer is very dark and dangerous - many monsterous creatures live there and few return to tell about it. The lower level is less mysterious, but is still full of hazardous wildlife and considered dangerous for outsiders. The upper layer is where many Elves, Halflings and Dalvas live, despite being between 4-5 kilometers off the ground on average. The typical travelling paths taken between towns and cities crossing the Veldt are done in between the upper and lower layers. Finally, the sky layer is at the top of the trees: home primarily to flying creatures large and small, only certain tribal peoples or pilgrims would take permanent residence their. In the high canopy, virtually nothing protects from the sun's hot, harsh rays. All accounted for, the Veldt reaches from the southwestern shores of the Violet Sea up to the central and eastern parts Azergad, stretching into the nations of Inazria, Avarazhan, Azelliot, and Zilwynbar (plus a handful of islands on the southern tip of the Kystriland chain).

The Veldt is a forest of impressively massive trees: they are wide enough to fit entire towns, potentially stand kilometers tall, and grow in close proximity to each other. Veldt-dwellers frequently live high up in the leaves and branches of the giant trees. This is because the forest floor tends to be cold, void of useful resources, and full of dangerous monsters.

Currently, the Veldt occupies the bulk of northern Avarazhan, thins out at the border between eastern Inazria and western Azelliot, and stretches through the foothills of southern Bisengal and western Zilwinbar.

### The Bashan Coast

Along the western edge of Avarazhan, the forest abruptly halts in an unbroken rampart of stone called the Bashan coast before picking up once again across the chaotic Asuran wildland islands. It is a scarp that runs the length of the coast, raw rock falling sheer into deep water with no beach to soften the meeting. The Veldt grows right to the lip and beyond. Its outermost giants overhang the void, roots clutching the cliff edge like fingers over a ledge, and over the centuries the eldest of them have toppled whole into the sea. The rains pool upon the hanging canopy, so the leaves must bleed into rivers, which gather kilometers overhead and spill off the Bashan edge in towering ribbons of falling water that scattering to mist long before reaching the Violet Sea underneath.

### The Asuran Isles

Westward, the drowned remnants of the same land breaks the surface as the Asuran isles. They appear as low-lying shattered ridges, the tallest still crowned with surviving Veldt giants gone strange and wild in their isolation. Accounts detail some of the most chaotic and untamed places in all Azergad.

## Avarazhan

To the south of the Veldt lies the coastal plateau region of Avarazhan. The land rises as a great uplifted plateau of ancient, stable rock - a perfect place to gaze beyond the stars on a typical clear night. To the south and west lie many rivers that cut through the edges of the plateau into stunning natural waterfall drains. The high rock gradually blends into canyons and flat-topped mesas as it travels eastward. Further east, the land here becomes drier and colder as the land lowers into stony hills and rocky beaches. As Avarazhan and Azelliot meet in the southeast, the warmth rapidly drained from the lands. The coast itself begins to split into glacial fjord country before the tundra emerges, announcing the begining of the edge of the world. Down here, the aurora burns in bands of emerald and sapphire beyond neverending frozen wasteland.

## The Kystrilands

West of Inazria lies the expansive Kystrilands, the largest island chain in the world. According to the Great Ledger, there are more than 20,000 islands spanning the longitude of several nations with varying degrees of civilization. Different islands are home to many different cultures and people groups. Northern islands tend to be sandy, rocky and volcanic, while southern islands tend to be tropical and heavily forested. The islands are loosely organized into rings: the inner 'ring' begins just off the coast of Windfall, arcing west and then to the south of the Violet sea. The outer ring is further away from the mainland, consist of numerous small to tiny islands, and is more spread out overall. The largest islands in the chain reside in the geographical center of the inner ring, which tend to be urbanised and are farthest away from mainland Inazria. The islands generally form an "arc" which faces the mainland. Islands on the outer ring and beyond tend to be extremely dangerous due to the presence of unpredictable natural activity and vastly untamed and large wildlife.

Kystriland's mountains stretch high yet deep underground and are filled with precious materials such as gold, pik (also known as white gold), and betharyl (a high-quality material used for ornate equipment). Most of the world's trade involves materials and logistics that flow through this region.

### Andasena

Far northwest of the Kystriland arc, across some of the most treacherous seas in the world, lies the island of Andasena. The Senawara, the island's great lake system, is scattered with small island chains and joined by rivers that plunge over towering waterfalls.

## The Violet Sea

The Kelanari are the people of the Violet Sea itself: the great inland ocean whose currents bind Kystriland, Inazria, and the southern reaches of the Veldt into one turning wheel of life. Kelanari society is among the oldest civilizations in Azergad. Its monuments are not towers, but cities grown and carved into living reef and sheer sea cliff; their flowing halls shaped so that the currents themselves move through them like breath.

### Azimar

The coral courts of Azimar, the reef capital, have hosted the embassies of every Realm - yet, they remain to be seen by most outsiders.

### Zerakahm

The mysterious Khemazi dwell in the deepest parts of the Violet Sea, in cities built within underwater trenches and abyssal plains. Their trench-city of Zerakahm is raised upon ruins as old as Azergad itself: the drowned remains of the first Kelanari settlements, sunk into the deep in an upheaval that far predates the Great Pact and its records.

### Floating Cities

The Pelagoran live aboard great floating cities that drift with the currents of the Violet Sea.

## Zilwynbar

Between the great plains of Azelliot to the south and the dry tundra of Bisengal to the north lies Zilwynbar, the smallest of the Realms yet the most abundant in life. The wetland terrain, which makes up the bulk of Zilwynbar's land, is the gift of the mighty Kerangala river, born of melting snow high in the Marram. The Kerangala runs east across the lowlands, fraying along the way into hundreds of lesser channels that wander across a flat basin no wider than a week's march. The land of Zilwynbar exude growth, creativity and abundance. Nowhere else in Azergad does so much press into so little; the warm, shallow water breeds a staggering diversity of crops, animals, birds, fish and, unfortunately, beasts as well. Scattered across the region's lakes and slow rivers stand the simple villages of the Limnari, the only Kelanari peoples to live outside the Violet Sea.

The Halflings of Zilwynbar inhabit the smallest of the Realms yet the most full of life, where the hundred channels of the Kerangala turn the lowlands into a sprawling, neverending garden.

The lakes of the Limnari open downward into the sunless, treacherous waterways that vein the earth below Azelliot and Zilwynbar - the same hidden waters credited for the astonishing fertility of both lands.

### The Fennveldt

In the southwest, the Veldt comes to its end. The titanic trees thin and stoop as the ground turns soft beneath them, until the last of the giants stand drowned to the knee in the small region called the Fennveldt. It is here where the vast forest finally loses its grip and the marsh claims what is left behind.

### The Aaldemar

Out east, the channels of the Aaldemar delta slow and turn brackish as they near the coast. The river and sea blur into one trembling expanse of salt and reed that belongs to neither — a coastline that spells treachery for sailors and traders of all kinds. In the Aaldemar, only the cunning, bravery and wit of the Aaldegar halflings could beat the region's beasts and terrain far enough back to make it their home.

### The Morybell Isles

Along the far eastern coast of Zilwynbar, beyond the brackish maze of the Aaldemar, a chain of small volcanic islands smokes on the horizon. These are the Morybell Isles, born in a colossal string of eruptions during the 1st Generation and restless ever since. They are home to the Keldale, the Halflings who plan around geology the way the Wendric plan around seasons. The largest island, where most of the Keldale live, is called Isle Kloenard. Their villages are set into warm rock and heated by the earth itself; steam vents cook their meals and flowing lava fires their forges. It's normal for eruptions to erase paths between towns, requiring new roads to be charted.

## Bisengal

Beyond the Marram, in the long rain-shadow of the same peaks whose snowmelt feeds the Kerangala, lies Bisengal — the expansive dry, windswept dominion of the Orcs. Bisengal is the largest single territory in all of Azergad, despite being sparsely populated even within its cities.

### The Targai

The heartland is Targai, a vast cold steppe of frostbitten grass and bare stone that rolls on farther than any traveler cares to walk. This is the country of the Dramkhani, the heartland orcs and heirs of the ancient Dramakha — nomads who follow their herds across the frostbitten grass, and the stock from which Karhu herself is sprung. Wind owns this land, carrying the sharp, guttural speech of the Orcs farther than any voice should reach.

### The Dramtal

The Dramtal scours the bones of fallen empires, strewn with the ruins of the Dramakha and the dynasties that chased its memory, half-swallowed now by dust and cold.

### Khurz-Marn

At its center stands Khurz-Marn, the citadel of Karhu, where the High Tribunal keeps the hardest law in Azergad. The city lies at the northern shore of Bisengal's salty sea, an outlet for the Marram mountain that never makes it to the harsh, cold shore. During the winter months, the sea freezes over completely.

### The Oztagh

To the south and east, the steppe withers into the Oztagh, a true desert of scorching days and freezing nights where the Gushbek hold sovereign territory — runners of the horizon who charted these wastes long before the clans claimed them, and who still keep towns in the northern wetlands of Zilwynbar. The harsh terrain relents only at the border, where the green channels of the Kerangala spill across into halfling country and an honored trade friendship endures.

### The Karatagh

Along the western and southern rim rise the Karatagh, the black peaks the Orcs took from the Hyzalians by the Treaty of Marram, where the Yaz'togal raise their fortress-cities in stride with the passes between nations.

### The Palcha Valley

Far to the west, where the range crumbles into a maze of steep-walled canyons, lies the secluded Palcha Valley, a lasting history of a people who once ruled this whole frontier. It is a hard country in every direction, but it's a harshness native Orcs boast of while grinning ear to ear.
