# Lore

The lands of Azergad are described in [Geography](geography.md).

Collectively, the nations are called the Realms of the Pact, bound together by the Great Pact signed at Thilidan nearly three thousand years ago. The Pact formalized their borders and set a framework for peace among the peoples.

The Paragons of Man had already led their peoples since the beginning. Though they rule their home nations, the Paragons give back to the Realms of the Pact by governing their own share of international responsibilities. Together, they have built a prosperous and peaceful society through the generations. 

Time in Azergad is counted in generations. The 1st Generation ended with the Treaty of Marram (890 1G). The 2nd and 3rd Generations each lasted a thousand years. The 4th Generation is underway, currently residing at the year 110 4G.

## The Paragons of Man

In the earliest days of Azergad, the ancient races were left to bring order to the world. Each race produced a sole individual to guide them through the new frontier. The immortal Paragons of Man are the purest of heart, the wisest in mind, and the highest in spirit.

The Paragons soon arranged a constitution that formalized the Realms of the Pact. The Great Pact formalized country barriers, created international roads for trade and travel, identified a common language separate from national languages, and established a loose guide for maintaining peace at large. The Seven Realms signed at Thilidan: the tropical peninsula of Inazria (inhabited by the Humans), the dry tundra of Bisengal (inhabited by the Orcs), the vast chain of islands called Kystriland (inhabited by the Dwarves), the deep dark woodlands of Avarazhan (inhabited by the Elves), the lowland marsh called Zilwynbar (inhabited by the Halflings), the vast Violet Sea (inhabited by the Kelanari), and the great plains of Azelliot (inhabited by the Dalvas). The Marram mountains, home to the Hyzalians, were set apart by the Marram Covenant and did not sign. That day signified the first day of the first month of the first Generation of time, where the earliest known records began.

Hours later, the peace was in shambles. The Hyzalian named Zhu'kal, the White-Cold Wizard, had discovered a power unlike anything ever seen before. His newfound strength was not magic as mankind had ever wielded, but something never before seen or understood. The violent snowstorm erupted over tropical Thilidan. Zhu'kal's intent was clear from the beginning: eliminate the Paragons at any cost. The Paragons hid as the wind and hail stayed strong for 3 dusks, while Zhu'kal and his zealous minions hunted them. On the third day, the Hyzalian Zaomyth alone confronted Zhu'kal, and tried to reason with him to halt the storm; Zaomyth's pleas, utterly ignored, were met with fierce combat. Zaomyth fought back, and after slaying the last of Zhu'kal's followers, the White-Cold Wizard cast a spell to banish Zaomyth to an unknown plane. The brutal storm had taken many lives across Azergad.

Finally, on the dawn of January 4th, 1 1G, a bright light washed across the landscape. In a flicker, the storm subsided. Zhu'kal and his followers were nowhere to be seen or heard from again.

Members of the Paragons were said to be the most noble and wise of their peoples, providing a combination of impressive characteristics and high regards in the bloodline. They seem to be immortal, guiding their followers still four generations later. The Paragons are undoubtedly the greatest leaders Azergad has ever seen.

Each Paragon embodies a distinct virtue, keeps their seat in their people's homeland, and leads an order of brave and noble fighters sworn to bear that virtue into the wider world. Someone who pledges their oath to serve at the hand of a Paragon is called a Paladin.

- **Narthex, Father of Peace** — The Paragon of the Humans resides at the Hall of First Light in Thilidan. He embodies patience and the conviction that violence must end where it began. He founded the Concord, a multinational Paladin order whose white-mantled knights act as neutral mediators on the treaty-roads of Azergad. Knights of the Concord swear to offer parley before steel. Chronicles credit Narthex with many major mediations since the Pact, including the Treaty of Marram. Common folk say he wept when Zaomyth was banished by Zhu'kal in the White-Cold storm.
- **Karhu, Queen of Justice** — The Paragon of the Orcs presides over the High Tribunal at the citadel of Khurz-Marn, in the dry tundra of Bisengal. She holds the highest judicial bench in Azergad, personally overseeing the justice done to Azergad's worst offenders. Her fervor for justice is famous, as is the swiftness of her verdicts (and her less-than-typical mercifulness). The Yargharu ride at her writ as judge, jury, and executioner against evils of many kinds. They hunt thieve's guilds, slave traders, oathbreakers, and rogue magisters who outrun their own nation's law. Karhu led the Bisengalian campaign that ended the Hyzal's place upon the Marram.
- **Valadorf, Keeper of the Law** — The Paragon of the Dwarves rules from the High Court of Kalindor in central Kystriland. He embodies precedent, order, and the sanctity of the sworn word. He maintains the Great Ledger, in which every signed accord of the four Generations is bound and recopied by hand, including the trade compacts and commercial codes that bind the Realms of the Pact. His Pillars of Kalindor answer to two sworn duties. The first is the physical defense of the Ledger and personal protection to the Paragons themselves. The second is the enforcement of commercial law across Azergad. Throughout Kystriland ports and counting-houses, a Pillar's drawn shield is the final word on smuggling and fraud.
- **Ashiera, Seer of Will** — The Paragon of the Elves dwells in Quietsong, beneath the deepest groves of Avarazhan. She embodies foresight, patience, and deep intellect through careful study. She rarely sits at the Council of Seven and is almost never seen by her Elves, let alone outsiders. Her closest allies say a single glance from her is said to read the will of the soul itself - even discovering what they have desperately hidden from themselves. Her Wardens of Will are an order in her own image, cloistered for decades of training and sworn to silence in public. They are loosed only against the threats that operate in the shadows and cross generations. It is said that the Wardens have foiled the plots of hidden cults, oaths seeped in secrecy, and the slow corruptions of court and crown. Their long lifespans and experience of past Generations also make them sought-after counsel.
- **Calrien, High Shepherd of Alore** — The Paragon of the Kelanari holds no fixed court, traveling across the wild reaches of Azergad as her duties demand. Her and her Shepherds regard the natural world itself as their ward. Her Shepherds answer to two sworn callings. The first is to rescue those in peril far from civilization, such as sailors lost at sea, caravans stranded by natural disasters, or travellers swallowed up by the Veldt. The second is to defend the natural wonders of Alore from those who would waste or despoil them.
- **Myrdin the Meek** — The Paragon of the Dalvas walks the open plains of Azelliot. Myrdin the Meek takes no permanent seat and refuses every title beyond his name. He embodies humility and the conviction that the smallest soul is worth the same as the highest. He publically speaks against evil when he sees it, and he especially reviles necromancy and the dark arts. The Sangoman travel across Azergad, healing and teaching the sick and poor while destroying every trace of evil they come across. They wear no heraldry, accept no payment, and never draw their blades first. 
- **Ostro, Author of Knowledge** — The Paragon of the Halflings resides at the Great Library at Zilwynbar. He embodies wisdom, memory, and counsel. Over the Generations he has gathered the world's knowledge, building his Library up as the foremost authority on every craft of Azergad, magical and otherwise. It also holds a copy of Azergad's every chronicle, which are read aloud each year at the annual New Solar Reading. The Arbiters are renowned for their intellect and serve as advisors to kings, magistrates, and adventuring companies. Senior Arbiters are also versed in certain arcane rites and workings drawn from the Library's deeper shelves. They are sworn never to withhold a true word in counsel, and never to speak a false one.

## Beyond the Paragons

For the nine centuries that followed, the Marram Covenant held the Hyzalian heights set apart from the Realms of the Pact. Trade and travel moved regularly through Marlis and the passes, and the lower slopes saw their share of incidents during this time. After over 900 years of development, numerous small wars and conflicts, and innovations in thought, Karhu sought to punish the Hyzalians for the acts of their ancestors. The Hyzalians were, and are even still, poorly understood. Seemingly lacking in technical and social progression, the Paragon-less Hyzalians clung to traditions and mystical, primal practices. As a culture, they do not trust outsiders and refuse outsider counsel.

Emboldened Orcish forces greatly outnumbered and outpaced the lackluster and disjointed Hyzalian military response, driving those few remaining Hyzal to the peaks of the highest Marram summits. Now greatly reduced in population and space, the Paragons met to herald peace once more. The Treaty of Marram, signed on the 11th day of the 10th month, 890 1G, redrew the country boundaries to expand Bisengal into parts of the Marram. In exchange for great military aid, and as a plea for help from the few remaining Hyzal, the lower slopes were divided into Bisengalian and Inazrian jurisdictions. The Hyzal remained in cloistered tribal communities atop the harsh mountain landscape, officially gaining lawful Inazrian or Bisengal citizenship, though Hyzalians would still live in self-imposed isolation even in the 110th year of the 4th Generation. Since the Treaty of Marram was orchestrated mostly without the input of the Hyzalians themselves, many deep scars from the conflict live on today. Due to the hastily drawn borders, thousands of communities and families became fragmented and at times are living at odds against each other. Many generations-old squabbles persist into the modern day, holding the Hyzalians further back from assimilating with Azergad culture. This great event marked the turn of the 1st Generation; each Generation since has lasted a thousand years, save the first.
