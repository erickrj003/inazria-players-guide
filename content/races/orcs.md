## Orcs

The Orcs of Bisengal are a diverse and hardy people shaped by the unforgiving landscapes they call home. Orcish civilization values strength, endurance, and unwavering loyalty to their clans. Orc society emphasizes personal honor achieved through overcoming adversity, whether in combat, survival, or mastery of one's craft. Because such honor must be earned rather than inherited, many young orcs leave Bisengal to seek a hardship worth conquering, like serving abroad as caravan guards, builders, tribunal deputies, or adventurers, before returning home to boast of their feats.

Orcs are powerfully built, standing around 6 and a half to 7 and a half feet tall with broad shoulders and muscular frames built on generations of physical adaptation. Their rough skin often ranges from deep forest green to weathered grayish-brown. Their prominent lower canines and strong jaws reflect a carnivorous heritage, while their keen senses serve them well in Bisengal's harsh environments. Orcs can live up to 120 years, but the typical lifespan is between 60-80 years. Orcs reach maturity by age 16.

Throughout the last thousand years, several Orcish empires have risen and fallen across Azergad. In antiquity, it was the Dramakha empire that first rose to prominence, asserting long-lasting dominance over the other races; the conquests of rising leaders ever since have all sought to regain their past glory. 

At one time, a revived Dramakha empire conquered vast swaths of Inazria and Zilwynbar through superior Orcish metallurgy and warfare tactics, only to fragment when the ruling clan was challenged by dozens of rival houses simultaneously. Another time, a reunified offensive built the greatest naval force the world had ever seen, controlling the major trade routes across the Violet Sea, but collapsed again when a succession crisis spiralled into the War of Burning Ships. As recently as the 4th generation, another echo of the Empire controlled large portions of Azelliot, Zilwynbar and northern Inazria. An empowered public protested, leading to deadly resistance efforts on many borders. As Dramakha power was weakening, the squabbles of the ruling class quickly erupted into familiar ethnic tensions between Gushbeks, Dramkhani and Yaz’togals. Yet again, a new conflict exploded into the devastating three-way civil war that has left the Orcish people broken, impoverished, and confined to the hostile badlands of Bisengal today.

### Traits

- **Ability Score Increase**: +2 Strength
- **Age**: Orcs mature by age 16 and live up to 120 years.
- **Size**: Orcs are larger and bulkier than humans. Your size is Medium.
- **Speed**: Your base walking speed is 30 feet.
- **Darkvision**: You can see in dim light within 60 feet as if it were bright light, and in darkness as if it were dim light in shades of gray.
- **Relentless Endurance**: When you are reduced to 0 hit points but not killed outright, you can drop to 1 hit point instead. You can't use this trait again until you finish a long rest.
- **Tempered Strength**: Once per short or long rest, when you hit a creature with a melee weapon attack, you can deal extra damage equal to your proficiency bonus, or twice your proficiency bonus if you are below half your hit point maximum.
- **Powerful Build**: Generations of labor, war, and survival in Bisengal's badlands have built frames to match. You count as one size larger when determining your carrying capacity and the weight you can push, drag, or lift.
- **Languages**: You can speak, read, and write Common and Orcish. Orcish is a deep, harsh, guttural language with sharp consonants that carries well across Bisengal's windswept landscapes. Orcish is a difficult language for non-Orcish peoples to speak (except for the Dalvas).

### Dramkhani

The Dramkhani are the heartland orcs of the Targai steppe and the heirs of the ancient Dramakha, the first empire to rule Azergad. The Dramkhani are traditionally semi-nomadic people, following the grass and the seasons across the frostbitten but fertile steppe. From among the Dramkhani come the rulers of Khurz-Marn, the capital and great city of Bisengal, and the Paragon Karhu herself. The Dramkhani peoples and clans are wide-reaching and powerful. To this day, the Dramkhani diaspora is among the most widespread across Azergad. It is common to find Dramkhani in positions of power wherever they go. 

Dramkhani are regal, tall and powerfully built, with weathered skin in deep forest-greens and grey-browns, dark, thin hair commonly worn in long, tight braids, and eyes of amber or brown.

- **Ability Score Increase**: +1 Charisma
- **Language**: You can speak Targun, the ancient tongue of the first Dramakha empire. In fact, modern Orcish descends directly from it. To speak Targun is to speak as the old emperors did, and fluency is considered the honorable mark of pure Orcish heritage.
- **Brutal Presence**: You have proficiency in the Intimidation skill, and you can make Intimidation checks using your Strength modifier in place of Charisma.
- **Life on the Move**: Your nomadic heritage equips you for an adventuring lifestyle. You have proficiency in Survival, and you gain advantage on Constitution saving throws made against suffering the effects of exhaustion.

### Gushbek

The Gushbek were once a roaming caste of traders, cattle herders and explorers who charted the untouched corners of rural Bisengal. At the height of the Dramakha empire, it was the Gushbek caste which caravaned goods and travelers across the treacherous Oztagh. This is the origin of their name: in the old Dramkhani tongue, Gushbek means "windswept runner." Their skills and knowledge of the Oztagh desert is unparalleled. 

For generations, The Dramakha royalty old regarded the Gushbek as a lesser people. As a result, they were (and still to this day) subjected to a degree of duress and persecution. When civil war at last fractured the Dramakha empire, intrepid Gushbek leaders claimed the whole of the Oztagh as sovereign land, including parts of Zilwynbar's northern wetlands. The Dramakha marched to take back their territory, but the Gushbek and their Wendric allies held their ground, again and again, often against far greater numbers. Accounts of those skirmishes are now modern fables: tales of unbelievably daring and cunning feats which have since passed into legend. In the years since, both sides have settled into an uneasy but mutually beneficial separation. 

Gushbek and Halfling relations continue to be among Azergad's most longstanding alliances. Among the many wandering caravans of Wendric traders, it is rare not to find Gushbek companions. Even amongst the crowds of Thilidan and Marlis, the two peoples can trust that family ties are likely to make fast friends out of strangers.

Gushbek stand tall and slender beside other Orcish kin, with tan and grey skin in many shades and eyes of gold, amber, or sky blue, complete with thick dark hair bound tightly to combat the wind and sand.

- **Ability Score Increase**: +1 Dexterity
- **Language**: You can speak Gushbek, a guttural language commonly used throughout rural Bisengal that is mutually unintelligible with standard Orcish. However, the language exists as a spectrum and is highly contextual, from near-Orcish in the northern wetlands to something closer to Uncommon trade-speech in the south. Merchants crossing the Kerangala learn a few phrases whether they mean to or not.
- **Desert Runner**: You are acclimated to the desert's extreme heat and freezing nights. You can go up to five days without drinking before you risk exhaustion from thirst, and you ignore difficult terrain caused by sand, dust, and loose rock. You can choose not to leave tracks on sandy or dusty surfaces.
- **Horizon Sense**: You have proficiency in Survival. After spending 1 minute observing open terrain, you know the cardinal directions, the rough hours until nightfall, and whether a sandstorm or similar hazard is likely within the next few hours. You cannot become lost in desert, steppe, or badland terrain except by magical effects.

### Barithani

The Barithani trace their roots to the Barith, the high Dramkhani clan who spearheaded the conquests of the first Dramakha empire. These people were particularly feared in their day for their reputation as bloodthirsty marauders that freely dabbled in forsworn magic. When the empire first fell, the surviving royal Dramkhani clans agreed to turn on the Barith out of fear, banishing them people from Bisengal entirely. Neither Inazria nor Zilwynbar would tolerate such barbaric people. After a 10 year period of wandering, the Barithani eventually fled to the one place no race of man would dare follow - it was the lightless lower depths of the Veldt, a hellish place called the Thograk. There, over thousands of years in perpetual twilight beneath the canopy, the Barithani became a new people of their own right. 

In the modern day, the Barithani are expert hunters and trappers who move in silence across the forest floor. They have learned to raise hidden settlements within root systems and fallen logs, and survive in no small part on the supplies they plunder from innocent travelers which stray too low off the Veldt's beaten paths.

Generations in the dark have reshaped them. Barithani skin has grown pale, ashy and desaturated to colors of ash-grey, ecru, beige, or light scarlets; their eyes burn red or amber and catch the faintest light; their features are sharp and angular, their fanged maws fuller than other Orcs, and their nails long and blackened. To the rest of the world, their cultivated reputation matches their fearsome appearance.

- **Ability Score Increase**: +1 Constitution
- **Language**: You can speak Barithani, a less elegant and quicker-sounding version of Dramkhani that borrows words and phrases from Veldtwin and Yorokai. Barithani is almost never learned or used beyond Barithani villages, but can be helpful for conveying simple messages to other Dramkhani, Veldtwin and Yorokai speakers.
- **Tracker's Sense**: You have proficiency in Stealth. You can track creatures that passed through an area up to 7 days ago, and you can identify familiar species, the creature's approximate size, and its direction of travel. When in forested terrain, you can attempt to hide even when only lightly obscured by foliage.
- **Eyes of the Deep Veldt**: Generations in the lightless depths have sharpened your sight in darkness but dulled it in the day. Your darkvision extends to 90 feet. However, while in bright light such as direct sunlight, you have disadvantage on Wisdom (Perception) checks that rely on sight.

### Yaz'togal

The Yaz'togal hold the highest peaks of the Marram, where their great fortress cities and watchtower networks guard the passes between nations. The Yaz'togal people trace their ancestry to an ancient Dramakha clan from the old empire. In antiquity, they were considered the world's first builders and engineers: a people embracing the cutting edge of a brief renaissance which developed out of Dramkhani hoarded wealth. After the Treaty of Marram, the Yaz'togal clan was given a lasting purpose: to fortify Bisengal's new mountain border and keep watch on the suspicious Hyzalians and Palcha peoples. In exchange, the Yaz'togal have earned their high seat in Kurz-Marn and Orcish society broadly. Where others might trust their own strength, the Yaz'togal prefer sharp eyes and strong foundations built from centuries of innovations in mathematics, masonry, and master craftsmanship. They are broad and weathered like all Orcish kind, but are marked by keen, deep-set eyes, downward-slanted brows and skin in the light greys and browns of the high rock they have made their home.

During the four centuries of Palcha imperialism of the 2nd Generation, Yaz'togal fortresses were a common safe haven for Yooqua (Palcha defectors). The Palcha wielded their hereditary telekinetic gift called Kalma as a weapon and a mark of superiority over the other races of men. Over decades of experimentation, Yaz-togal and Yooqua researchers discovered that Kalma could be reduced to notation and taught to anyone with the discipline to study it. In fact, they discovered that Kalma, as practiced by the Palcha, was a limited form of the practice, and those who really understood the craft could wield it to a much higher potential. They called the result Bek'tan. Bek'tan proved to be the instrumental craft that pried western Bisengal back from Palcha hands one fortress at a time, culminating at the critical Battle of Three Quakes. After the war, the Yaz'togal learned to apply Bek'tan toward more practical uses like construction, which they are most known for in modern times.

- **Ability Score Increase**: +1 Intelligence
- **Language**: You can speak Yaz'togal, a difficult but pleasant sounding dialect of Orcish. Many know basic Yaz'togal because of its wide applications as a trade language all across the Marram. Everyday usage is typically associated with the upper and royal classes of Yaz'togal society - speaking it proficiently is a sign of status.
- **Tower Code**: You know the horn-and-banner signal code of the Yaz'togal watchtowers. You can send and understand simple messages to anyone within line of sight who knows the code, no matter the distance. You can teach the basic code to a willing creature over a week of daily, 1-hour practice sessions, allowing it to exchange simple messages with you in this way.
- **Built to Last**: You have proficiency in Perception, mason's tools and carpenter's tools. With a glance, you can tell whether a structure is sound, sabotaged, or recently built or altered. Furthermore, when you spend 10 minutes or more reinforcing a door, wall, barricade, or other structure, creatures have disadvantage on ability checks made to break through it, and it has advantage on saving throws against being damaged or destroyed.
